How the checking works

Methodology

A mark is only useful if you know exactly what it covers. Here is what we look at, what we copy, and where the public record runs out.

Clean-verified means no corporate PAC and no corporate contributions were found in the records we checked. It is not a promise about everything a company does.

01What clean-verified means

Clean-verified means that our review found no corporate PAC and no corporate contributions in the public records we checked for the election cycle identified on that company's page.

This is a specific finding based on specific records. It is not a guarantee that a company has no political involvement, that its executives make no personal political contributions, or that no political spending occurs through organizations outside the records we review.

We identify the sources and the cycle checked on each company page so you can see what supports the label and understand its limits. Our job is to make the records accessible and understandable. Your job is to decide what they mean to you.

02The three marks

  • Clean-verified No corporate PAC and no corporate contributions found in the records checked.
  • Flagged A corporate PAC was found in the records checked. Where the record gives an FEC committee ID, we show it and link to that committee on fec.gov. One company stays flagged on an older catalog record even though no committee for it appears in the current FEC registry; its page explains that plainly.
  • Not yet verified Checking started, but something is still open, such as a company that files under more than one legal name. These are never shown as clean, and each page says what is left to check.

03What we check

  • FEC records. Corporate PACs register with the Federal Election Commission and report what they raise and spend.
  • OpenSecrets. We use OpenSecrets summaries of those public filings.
  • Company policy. Where a company has stated a policy about PACs, we note it.

Each company page names the source used and the election cycle checked.

04How we quote

Every company page carries a quote block, and the label says where the words came from. Where we could open the source itself, the quote is that source word for word: a PAC's registered name as the FEC record shows it, or a company's own policy sentence. Otherwise the quote is the line from the record our catalog cites. We do not tidy any of it up, and we never write policy sentences or committee names that were not in the record. If a record is still being re-verified, the page says so and quotes nothing.

Links follow one rule. If we have confirmed a company's FEC committee ID, the link goes to that committee's page on fec.gov for the 2023-2024 cycle. If the quote comes from a company's own page, the link goes there. Otherwise it goes to the source's home page, and the company page says so plainly, so nobody mistakes it for a page about that company.

05How we show amounts

For flagged companies we show only figures confirmed in the FEC record for the committee we link to, with the FEC's own labels, such as Total receipts or Contributions to other committees. Until a committee's 2023-2024 totals are confirmed, the page shows no number and no estimate, and says why.

06When we last checked

Wave checked . Each new wave adds companies and carries its own date, and a recheck moves the date forward.

07How corrections work

If something looks wrong or out of date, send it through the Suggest a Company / Corrections form. A source helps a great deal: an FEC filing, a company statement, anything we can read for ourselves. We recheck against the public record, and if the record supports a change, we update the page and its date.

What we cannot see

Dark money. Some political spending moves through groups that never have to name their donors. Public records cannot show it, so neither can we.

Executives' personal giving. When an executive writes a check from their own account, that is their choice. It does not count against the company.

Anything outside the records above. If it is not in the filings we check, it is not part of the mark.
